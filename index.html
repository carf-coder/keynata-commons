    About

    Keynata Commons is the open music library from the maker of Keynata, the piano typing game. All tracks are composed by a rule-and-statistics music engine built from scratch — no neural networks, no training on copyrighted recordings. The engine learns broad stylistic statistics from public-domain scores and openly licensed data, writes music theory into explicit, sourced rules, and generates every piece deterministically from a random seed.

    New tracks are added in batches. Each release includes WAV audio, the MIDI file, and full generation metadata, so you can see exactly how each piece was made.

    How we keep it clean

    1. No copyrighted works go in.

      Style rules come from music theory and published common knowledge, each with a written source, kept in an audited ledger. Statistical models use only public-domain original scores and CC-licensed data.

    2. Similarity screening before release.

      Every track is checked against the training corpus for distinctive matching passages. Tracks that share any rare passage with a specific corpus piece are rejected and never published.

    3. No imitation objective.

      The engine has no mechanism that targets any specific song or artist.

    4. Honest limits.

      Coincidental resemblance to some existing melody can never be ruled out completely; screening reduces the risk, it does not eliminate it. This page describes our process and is not legal advice.

    5. Takedown policy.

      If you believe a track reproduces protected material despite the process above, open an issue on the GitHub repository with the track ID and the work you believe is affected. We will investigate and remove the track while the question is open. Removed tracks stay removed.
